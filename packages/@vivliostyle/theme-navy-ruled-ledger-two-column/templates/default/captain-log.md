### Master's Remarks {#rem-14mar}

Offshore passage from Port Calderwick to Vantross Haven continues in a generally
westerly flow. Conditions varied between fresh gale and near gale throughout the day
with a long northwest swell. Barometric pressure showed a gradual decline reaching
1008.7 hPa at 1400 before a slight recovery by midnight. Course adjusted at 0800 to
maintain the central track of the great-circle route laid down in the passage plan.

Vessel and cargo handling well in the present sea state. No machinery defects reported
by the Chief Engineer. Crew sound and attentive; no injuries or near-misses logged.

- Maintain 310° until the 34°W meridian, then reassess against the Kerrow Bank routeing.
- Secure all weather-deck lashings before the 0400 watch; second inspection at 1200.
- Report to Harrow & Blythe Navigation Co. at 0800, or earlier if the barometer falls
  below 1005 hPa.

### Master's Remarks {#rem-15mar}

The ridge promised in yesterday's routeing message moved over the vessel during the
forenoon and the sea went down to slight, with a low westerly swell only. Barometer
rose steadily to 1018.9 hPa in the second dog watch. Air temperature fell some three
degrees on the northerly airstream that followed the ridge across.

Altered to 280° at 1218 on crossing the 34°W meridian, as directed in
<a href="#rem-14mar" data-ref="sec"></a>, the Kerrow Bank routeing being clear for the
season. Made good 318 miles at 13.3 knots, the best day's run of the passage so far.
Propeller slip 9.0 per cent.

- Bunker sounding at 0600: 898.1 t of fuel oil remaining on board.
- Steering gear tested at 0930 and the emergency generator run for thirty minutes.
- A depression is reported forming to the southwest; routeing advice expected at 0600.

### Master's Remarks {#rem-16mar}

Barometer fell 18.2 hPa over the day as the depression reported yesterday deepened and
tracked northeast toward the vessel. Wind veered steadily from NNE through E to SE and
freshened from 9 to 28 knots, with a rising southeasterly sea by the last dog watch.
Air temperature rose to 12.6 °C in the warm air drawn ahead of the front.

Owners informed at 1510 as required by <a href="#rem-14mar" data-ref="sec"></a>, the
barometer having passed below 1005 hPa. Course altered progressively to 262° through the
afternoon to bring the sea onto the port quarter and to hold the vessel in the navigable
semicircle. Hatch covers, air pipes and deck lashings inspected and secured by 1740.

- All weather-deck work suspended from 1800; no person forward without the watch
  officer's permission.
- Call the master for any mean wind above 40 knots, or if pitching becomes heavy.
- Second securing round of the deck lashings at 2200, and bilges sounded at 2300.

### Master's Remarks {#rem-17mar}

The centre of the depression passed some ninety miles to the northward between 0800 and 1000. Barometer reached a minimum of 990.4 hPa in the forenoon watch and the wind a mean
of 41.2 knots, force 9, from the southwest, with a rough confused sea and heavy spray
over the forecastle. The cold front crossed at about 1420, after which the wind veered
sharply to west, the barometer began to rise and the air temperature fell 6.7 degrees in
four hours.

Revolutions reduced to 96 at 0240 and course eased to 250° to take the sea further on
the quarter. Made good 245 miles only, propeller slip 18.9 per cent. The vessel laboured
but shipped no green water, and no damage was found on the rounds at 1600 and 2200.

- Master on the bridge from 0200 to 1600; rest reduced to 9 h 00 m, recorded in
  <a href="#rem-crew" data-ref="sec"></a>.
- All bilges, the forepeak and the chain locker sounded at 1600 and 2200; no ingress.
- Resume full revolutions when the sea allows. Pass the revised arrival time to
  Vantross Haven by 2000.

### Master's Remarks {#rem-18mar}

Sea moderated through the middle watch and full revolutions were resumed at 0305.
Landfall obtained by radar at 0340 at thirty-eight miles. Pilot embarked at 0518, first
line ashore at 0709 and all fast starboard side to No. 4 berth at 0742. Free pratique
granted at 0836.

Arrival draughts 11.86 m forward and 12.04 m aft. Hatches Nos. 1 to 5 opened at 0950
after the surveyor's inspection and discharge commenced at 1024 with two shore cranes.
10,070 tonnes landed by midnight, leaving 31,180 tonnes on board.

- Cargo gear and hold ladders inspected with the stevedore foreman before work began.
- Deck watch to sound the ballast tanks and tend the moorings every two hours.
- Bunkers and stores ordered for 20 March; agent advised of the crew change on the 21st.

### Remarks on Crew & Safety {#rem-crew}

All hands mustered at the after station at 0700 and the full complement of twenty
answered their names. The nine ratings not named in
<a href="#tbl-muster" data-ref="tbl"></a>&nbsp;mustered at station B under the Bosun.
Lifejackets and immersion suits were inspected at the same muster; two lights were
renewed and one whistle replaced.

Hours of rest were within the limits of STCW A-VIII/1 for all hands save the master,
whose rest on 17 March was reduced to 9 h 00 m by the gale described in
<a href="#rem-17mar" data-ref="sec"></a>. The exception is recorded here and in
<a href="#tbl-rest" data-ref="tbl"></a>, and compensating rest was taken between 1200
and 1800 on 18 March.

- Fire and boat drill held 14 March at 1015; all hands, thirty-four minutes.
- Enclosed-space entry and rescue drill held 16 March at 1030, brought forward from the
  17th on account of the weather.
- Steering gear and emergency generator tested 15 March and again before arrival.

<p class="sign">SIGNED, MASTER</p>

### Master's Certification {#rem-cert}

I certify that the entries in sections <a href="#rec-14mar" data-ref="sec"></a>&nbsp;to
<a href="#rem-crew" data-ref="sec"></a>&nbsp;are a true record of the passage of this
vessel from Calderwick to Vantross Haven, and that they were made at the time of the
events recorded or as soon afterwards as the circumstances allowed. Positions are
observed at noon and were checked against two independent systems on each day.

Distance run from noon on 13 March to the pilot station, 1,373 miles at a mean of 12.1
knots. Consumed over the five days 121.8 tonnes of very low sulphur fuel oil and 9.5
tonnes of marine gas oil, leaving 829.6 and 61.4 tonnes respectively on board at
midnight on 18 March.

- No accident, injury, pollution incident or machinery breakdown occurred in the period.
- All statutory certificates were valid throughout and none expires before 30 September.
- Section closed at Vantross Haven on 18 March and forwarded to Harrow & Blythe
  Navigation Co. with <a href="#tbl-abstract" data-ref="tbl"></a>&nbsp;and
  <a href="#tbl-engine" data-ref="tbl"></a>.

<p class="sign">SIGNED, MASTER</p>
