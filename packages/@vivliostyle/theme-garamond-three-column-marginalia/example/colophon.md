# A Note on This Edition

How the sesquicentennial volume was made, and by whom.

This history was commissioned by the Delegates of the Press in June 2022 and written between then
and the autumn of 2025 by a committee of four under the editorship of Helena Sarre, Keeper of the
Press Archive. Chapters <a href="before-the-imprint.html#before-the-imprint" data-ref="chapter"></a>&nbsp;and <a href="press-proposed.html#the-press-proposed" data-ref="chapter"></a>&nbsp;are the work of the editor; Chapter <a href="academic-history.html#the-founding-decades" data-ref="chapter"></a>&nbsp;was drafted by
Ambrose Ketley and revised in committee; Chapters <a href="rationed-years.html#paper-war-and-retrenchment" data-ref="chapter"></a>&nbsp;and <a href="long-expansion.html#the-long-expansion" data-ref="chapter"></a>&nbsp;are by Ruth Ellingham; and
Chapter <a href="hot-metal-to-markup.html#the-digital-transition" data-ref="chapter"></a>&nbsp;is by the editor and Kwame Selby jointly. The appendices were compiled from the Press Archive by Ruth Ellingham, who
also checked the figures in every table against the annual returns.

The archive on which the book rests has been in the Bibliotheca since 1969 and was catalogued, with
a thoroughness the present writers have had constant reason to be grateful for, between 1974 and 1981. The Diary of the Printing Office, the Apprentice Book, and the minute books of the Delegates
are quoted throughout by their archive references; a fuller note on the sources, with the
concordance of old and new shelfmarks, is printed in the second impression.

Three editorial decisions may be worth recording, since the volume will outlast the reasons for
them. The first was to write the history from the day-book rather than from the catalogue, which
is why the reader will find more about paper, wages, and premises than about the books for which the
press is known outside the university; the second was to name the eleven holders of the office and
almost nobody else, not because the rest did not matter but because the records that would let us
name them fairly do not survive in equal measure across the hundred and fifty years, and a partial
roll of honour is worse than none. The third was to keep the fictions of the archive visible: where
a figure is an estimate it is called one, and where a minute is the only evidence for an event the
text says so.

<aside>The committee twice considered and twice rejected a chapter of reminiscence by former staff, on the ground that it would have covered only the years since 1975.</aside>

The volume’s dates require one caution. The press has counted its years from the sealing of the
statute on 9 May 1876, and the sesquicentenary accordingly falls in 2026; the university, which
counts the press from the appointment of its first Printer in the following August, has twice
celebrated three months late. The Delegates have declined, on each occasion, to adjudicate.

The text is set in EB Garamond, a modern revival of a sixteenth-century old-style face, with
headings in Libre Caslon Text and running heads, folios, and tabular matter in Archivo. The measure
is a three-column grid on a two-hundred-and-ten-millimetre page, which is narrower than the press
would ordinarily use for continuous prose and was chosen deliberately: it is the proportion of the
double-column octavo that the house used for its own reports from 1910 until the war, and the
volume is in that small respect a reprint of its own subject.

The figures throughout are line drawings made for this edition from originals in the Press Archive:
<a href="academic-history.html#fig-printing-house" data-ref="fig"></a>&nbsp;from the wood engraving of about 1878 in the Diary of the Printing Office,
<a href="academic-history.html#fig-fennel-row-works" data-ref="fig"></a>&nbsp;from the surveyor’s elevation of 1908, and <a href="chronology.html#fig-output-by-decade" data-ref="fig-appendix"></a>&nbsp;from the annual returns.
The decade totals in <a href="chronology.html#fig-output-by-decade" data-ref="fig-appendix"></a>&nbsp;and in <a href="office-of-printer.html#tbl-printers-and-directors" data-ref="tbl-appendix"></a>&nbsp;are drawn from the same series and
agree; the periods in <a href="academic-history.html#tbl-titles-published" data-ref="tbl"></a>&nbsp;do not correspond exactly to the decades of the chart, since the press counted its first period from the
sealing of the statute and its last from the semicentenary, and the eleven-year period 1916–1926 is
the consequence.

The paper is a cream wove of ninety grammes, made without optical brighteners, and the volume is
sewn in sections and bound in cloth over boards. Two thousand copies have been printed for the
sesquicentenary, of which two hundred and fifty are numbered and carry a leaf from the last sheet
printed at Gattersby Road in 1983. A digital edition is available without charge under the terms
described in Chapter <a href="hot-metal-to-markup.html#the-digital-transition" data-ref="chapter"></a>.

The editor’s thanks are due to the Keeper of the Bibliotheca and her staff, who produced the minute
books forty-one times without complaint; to the three surviving former Directors, who read the
chapters covering their own tenures and corrected nine errors between them, three of which they
had themselves introduced into the record at the time; to the Delegates, who commissioned a history
and then declined, when offered the opportunity, to read it in draft; and to the descendants of two
of the eleven, who lent family papers on the single condition that nothing in them be quoted
without the whole of the sentence it stood in.

Errors of fact should be reported to the Keeper of the Press Archive, who undertakes to correct them
in the digital edition within the month and in the second impression, should there be one, without
undertaking anything about the date.
