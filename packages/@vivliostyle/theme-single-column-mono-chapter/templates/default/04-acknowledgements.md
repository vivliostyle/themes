# Acknowledgements #

My first and greatest debt is to my supervisor, Professor Imogen Thackwray, who has read every
chapter of this dissertation more often than either of us would care to count, and whose insistence
that a stratigraphic argument must be legible in the field before it is legible on paper has shaped
the whole of the work. I am grateful also to my adviser, Dr Elias Bramfitt, for many hours of
patient argument about the limits of radiometric chronology.

The fieldwork would have been impossible without the crew of the survey launch _Curlew_, and in
particular without Mr Torin Ellesby, who found the sill at Alderford Narrows on the first attempt
after two of us had spent a fortnight failing to. Ms Nerys Calderbank of the Ardenlea Radiometric
Facility prepared and counted the <sup>210</sup>Pb samples, and explained the counting statistics to
me with a good humour that I did not always deserve. The Wrensea Hydrographic Office supplied the
tide-gauge archive for Alderford Quay and granted the permits under which the cores were raised.

This research was funded by a Graduate Studentship of the Halversham Trust, with additional
fieldwork support from the Kelmsleigh College Travel Fund and from the Marlbrook Sedimentology
Panel. I am grateful to all three bodies.

To my parents, and to Frances, who has lived with the Alderford Estuary for four years without ever
having seen it: thank you.
